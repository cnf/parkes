Rotator (rotctld)

Parkes starts and supervises rotctld itself when "Managed" is on. Model list comes from rotctl -l on this machine's Hamlib install. "Host" is always where this app connects; "Bind address" only matters when Managed is on -- it's what interface the daemon Parkes spawns listens on. Set it to 0.0.0.0 to let other rotctld clients (e.g. gpredict on a laptop) reach it over the LAN while Host stays localhost for Parkes's own connection.

Firmware (ESP32)

Updates the EasyCommII board's app firmware over its USB serial connection. Build DishControll, then select the app image (e.g. Arecibo.bin, from its build/ output) below -- only the app partition is rewritten, not the bootloader or partition table. rotctld is stopped automatically for the duration of the flash (freeing the serial port) and restarted once it finishes, whether it succeeds or fails. 


      

    


    

      

        
ESP32 Settings

        
      

      

        Firmware calibration/limit settings, read and written directly on the board's NVS storage.
        Not reachable yet -- no transport is wired up between Parkes and the board for this (its
        own Wi-Fi access point works today, but was ruled out to avoid an extra radio next to the
        SDR front end; a serial protocol sharing the same USB link as EasyComm is the likely
        answer, but doesn't exist in the firmware yet).
      

      

    


    

      

        

          
GPS (gpsd)

          
        

        
      

      

        Off by default -- most setups rely on the system's own gpsd.service, which other things
        (like chrony) may also use. Only turn "Managed" on for a Pi where Parkes is the sole gpsd
        consumer. "Host" is always where this app connects; "Bind address" only matters
        when Managed is on (anything other than localhost tells gpsd to listen on
        all interfaces, not just loopback).
